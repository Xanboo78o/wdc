KEY LADDER PEDALS

Cardboard + Nerf darts + your MX Keys Mini. A fake pressure sensor made of on/off switches.

01The trick

The game just counts how many of the three keys are down, so it doesn't matter which piece is over which key.

02You need

📦 Corrugated cardboard (a delivery box)
🎯 Nerf darts (about 4, foam part only, not the rubber tip)
🔫 Hot glue (or strong double-sided tape)
🩹 Masking tape (for the hinges)
📏 Ruler + scissors (craft knife with a parent)
⌨️ MX Keys Mini, switched on

03From above

Each flap hangs off the side of the keyboard. The return spring (a few dart pieces standing side by side) and the stop go under that overhang, standing on the base board, NOT on keys.

04From the side

The riser lifts the hinge so the flap clears the front rows of keys. Only the dart pieces reach down to the keyboard.

05Build it

  1. Base. Cut a board about 35 × 25 cm. Tape the keyboard onto it with masking tape on the edges (not the keys) so it can't slide.
  2. Flaps. Cut each flap from two layers glued together, with the corrugation lines crossed. That makes it stiff instead of bendy. Brake ≈ 15 × 20 cm, throttle ≈ 20 × 20 cm.
  3. Risers. Glue stacked cardboard strips about 25 mm tall in front of the keyboard (your side). Tape each flap's back edge to its riser with masking tape. That's the hinge.
  4. Dart pieces. Pull off the rubber tips and cut the foam into 6 pieces with flat, straight ends: two each at 20, 15 and 10 mm long. Stand them on end and glue them to the flap (never to the keys), right above 1 2 3 (brake) and 8 9 0 (throttle). Low-heat glue or double-sided tape: very hot glue can melt the foam.
  5. Spring. Under each flap's overhang, stand 3 or 4 dart pieces side by side (the same length) and glue them to the base. Pick a length that holds the flap up so key 1 is just not touching. More pieces = a stiffer pedal.
  6. Stop. Beside it, a stack of cardboard as tall as the flap should be when it's pushed a little past the third click. The flap lands on this, not on the keys.
The stop is not optional. Your leg can push way harder than a key is built for. The stop takes the force, so the keyboard only ever feels the foam.

06Tune it with the key tester

  1. Open the key tester (localhost:8176/keytest.html).
  2. Rest your foot on the flap without pushing. The bar should stay at 0%. If it shows 33%, trim the tallest dart piece a bit, or use longer spring pieces.
  3. Push slowly. You want it to climb 33 → 67 → 100% spread across the push. If two steps come at almost the same time, make those two pieces more different in length.
  4. Push as hard as you can. Still 100%, and the flap is resting on the stop? Done.
If a long piece bends over sideways instead of squashing straight, cut it shorter and make the flap sit a bit lower instead.
The foam squashing is what makes it feel like pressure: the further down you go, the more pieces you're squashing, so you have to push harder. That's how real brake pedals feel.
The MX Keys goes to sleep if you don't touch it for a while, and the first press just wakes it up. Tap a pedal once before you start a lap.

07Later: the clutch

A third flap. Shift keys usually don't count toward the 6-key limit, so they're the likely free keys for it (the key tester can check). The game needs an actual clutch first (bite point, launches, stalling), so that's a separate job.
